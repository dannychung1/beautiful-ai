Introduction

Every day, professionals face blank slides with something to say but no good way to say it. They have the idea, the data, the story. What they don't have is an afternoon to fight with text boxes.

Beautiful.ai does the design thinking for them so the person presenting is the smartest thing in the room, not the software that got them there.

This is the operating manual for the Beautiful.ai brand: how we look, sound, and behave across every surface.

BRAND TAGLINE

One phrase that encompasses our promise and point of view.

Workready.

Two words, two promises. Your work is ready for the rigors of real work — professional decks that hold up in the room they were built for. And your team stays on brand, always, because the guardrails travel with the work instead of living in someone's head.

That is how teams present smarter: start from a topic, an outline, or a document you already wrote, and get there 10x faster — together, in one place, with no second pass and no guessing.

Storytelling

Three stories nest inside each other. Brand is the outer layer and always on, product sits inside it whenever the platform is the clearest way to tell that story, and content is the innermost layer — the slide itself.

Brand Product Content
BrandOuter layer · always on

The professional's success. Every surface is telling this story, whether or not the product itself ever appears in frame.

ProductMiddle layer · often, not required

How the platform gets them there. Shown whenever a feature or the editor itself is the clearest way to tell the brand story — but brand doesn't need product visible to land.

ContentInner layer · the slide itself

The actual slide on screen. A clear, beautifully designed piece of content proves the brand promise on its own, with or without the product visible around it — most often shown together with product, since making the deck is the product's whole job, but not always.

Do

Four rules that keep it right.

Highest contrast

Always pick the logo version with the most contrast against its background.

1× clearspace

Keep clearspace equal to the height of the "b" on all four sides.

80px minimum

Never reproduce the wordmark narrower than 80px wide.

10% of the frame

The logo should occupy no more than a tenth of any composition.

Don't

Eight ways to break the signature.

The wordmark is a system, not a toy. Don't distort or tilt it, don't remake or recolor it, don't add effects, don't combine it with other logos, don't set our name as text inside a sentence — write "Beautiful.ai" — and never place it on a low-contrast background.

Display tier

Set display type at 95% leading and −15 tracking.

Display is the one tier above the scale — covers, chapter openers, and statement pages at 120px and up. It runs tighter than H1 on both axes: 95% line height and −0.035em tracking. Do not take the leading below 95%. Cap height is 0.70em and descenders drop 0.213em, so anything under 91% collides a descender with the caps on the line beneath it.

Optical alignment

Left-align display type to the ink, not the text box. Pull each line left by its own glyph side bearing — roughly 0.08em for B, 0.05em for G — so the stems land on the grid.

Open at small.

Directional light. Always facing what's next.

Composition

Single subject, room to breathe. Eye line at the rule of thirds, never centered like a passport photo.

Lighting

Directional and motivated — a window, a screen, a spotlight. Never flat, even fill. The subject sits in the light and faces it.

Crop

16:10 cinematic for brand. 4:3 for product. 3:4 portrait for content. One crop per layer, never mixed.

Diversity

Wide in role, age, geography. We design for everyone whose week ends in a deck.

Art direction

Iconography

We use the Material Symbols icon library, outlined and lightly rounded. No emoji, no decorative illustration, no hand-drawn glyphs. Consistency over cleverness.

Style

Outlined, rounded variant. Optical size 24. Stroke weight 400.

Sizes

24px is the standard. 20px in dense UI. 48px for feature icons.

Color

Inherit text color by default. Cyan only when calling for action.

The Savvy Sage is defined in the brand core. Imagery is how we render that character on the page — not in pictures of people, but in the marks, motifs, and surfaces we carry from slide to surface to story.

Archetype · One sentence
We are The Savvy Sage.

Beautiful.ai brings applied intelligence to work that solves real problems. The character that shows up before we say a word is usefully wise and wired for action — the steady hand that rethinks, refines, and rebuilds a draft into something fundamentally better.

Expressions

Three ways the archetype shows up.

These aren't photographs — they're the visual vocabulary the archetype carries from one surface to the next. Use them as the building blocks for imagery before reaching for any image at all.

Motif 01 — The dot

The moment of finish.

The cyan period from the wordmark, multiplied. A pattern of small acts of finishing — used as decoration only when it earns a meaning.

Motif 02 — The mark

Quiet at every scale.

The mark stays itself from favicon to billboard. The same proportions, the same dot. Confidence that doesn't need volume.

Motif 03 — Resolution

From rough to ready.

A visual idiom we own: a progression from raw to resolved, ending in the brand's cyan. Every story we tell ends in finished work.

Lean abstract

Geometric, type-driven, motif-led. We don't illustrate; we compose.

Lean still

Static and considered. Motion only when it pays off the moment.

Lean spare

One idea per surface. Negative space is the loudest tool we have.

Lean cyan

Save the accent for the moment of finish. Every other element is in service.

Which layer am I in?Brand, product, or content? Voice and density follow.

Is cyan doing one job?Highlight one action per surface. Save it for what matters.
