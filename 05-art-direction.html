Art direction

Our art direction exists to shine a flattering light on professionals in their element. We tell their story with photography, alongside our product and the slide stories they build in it. We never use illustration or cartoons to tell that story.

Art direction

Photography

We shine a flattering light on real professionals doing real work, bearing witness to their wins and revealing their process. As an integral part of their work, our product is always in frame.

Always in the light

We light the professional, literally and figuratively.

The professional's face is turned toward the light, and the same light lands on the wall behind them, so the whole frame points back to the person.

The professional is the hero and the product is implied, never shown. We shoot the way a trusted co-worker sees you: from across the table, mid-conversation, caught rather than posed.

The rules

These hold for every image and every shot type. Break one and the image doesn't ship.

01Light. The subject faces the light, and the brightest, warmest skin in the frame is on their face. A soft pool of the same light falls on the wall behind their head and shoulders. Windows and lamps may glow brighter than the face, and the person still reads as the lit point.
02Camera. Shoot from seated table height, through something soft in the foreground such as a table edge, a plant, a chair back, or glassware. We are in the room rather than observing it.
03Gaze. The professional looks at a colleague or at the task, just out of frame. Never at the lens.
04Together shots. One person is clearly primary and that person faces the light. The light goes to the professional the story is about, not automatically to whoever is speaking. Others can be side-lit or held in partial shade.
05Wardrobe. Tailored and professional, in quiet neutrals: deep navy, charcoal, camel, oatmeal, white. Add one small accent per image in Agency Azul or Ready Rose, never both, and keep it to about 5% of the frame. No logos and no loud patterns. Skip the magenta accent in any frame that already carries a magenta light cast.
06Devices. A laptop can sit in frame as part of the work, and it is never the focus. The professional stays the subject, and screen content is never readable. Lids are matte Base Blue #002533 and plain.
07Props and text. Coffee or tea, never alcohol. Any signage or writing in the background stays soft and illegible.
08Composition. Hold a single subject with room to breathe. Place the eye line on a third, never centered like a passport photo.
09Crop. Use 16:10 for brand, 4:3 for product, and 3:4 for content. Keep 1 crop per layer and never mix them.
10Range. Cast wide across role, age, and geography, so every professional who builds a deck sees their team in our frames.

01 · Core, fixed
A documentary editorial photograph of [SUBJECT], [ACTION], in [SETTING].
Photographed from seated table height through a softly blurred foreground element.
Gaze on a colleague or task just out of frame.

Portrait:       Shot on 85mm at f/2, chest-up, background softly out of focus.

Environmental:  Shot on 35mm at f/4, subject small in the lower third, open wall above for a headline.

Output, core prompt plus working shot

Before it ships

Every answer is yes. One no and the image goes back.

  • Is the primary subject's face the brightest, warmest skin in the frame?
  • Are they facing the light rather than backlit or silhouetted?
  • Is there a soft pool of light on the wall behind them, without a glowing halo?
  • Are they looking at a person or a task rather than the lens?
  • Is there a blurred foreground element?
  • Is there exactly one accent color, and is it small?
  • Is any laptop secondary to the person, with no readable screen?
  • Is all background text illegible, and is there no alcohol?
  • Do hands, eyes, and faces hold up at 100%?

Check the wall light first on every new batch. Generators either skip it or overdo it into an aura.

References, usage, and versions

Style references hold the grade better than any wording.

Reference pack

Use the official pack as --sref in Midjourney, a style reference in Firefly, or an image input in Gemini, GPT Image, and Flux. The pack is 8 to 10 of our own approved images, never another brand's photography. The prompt and checklist are open to anyone, and the brand team controls the reference pack.

Create, don't impersonate

We use no real person's likeness, and we never attach a customer name or quote to a generated face. Generated imagery in public-facing work follows our AI disclosure policy.

Art direction

Iconography

Icons come from Material Symbols, outlined and lightly rounded. No emoji, no hand-drawn glyphs. Where a single icon can't explain the idea, we draw a spot illustration in the same vocabulary — never a third style.

Style

Outlined, rounded variant. Optical size 24. Stroke weight 400.

Sizes

24px is the standard. 20px in dense UI. 48px for feature icons.

Color

Inherit text color by default. Cyan only when calling for action.
