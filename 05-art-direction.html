Art direction

We show professionals in their element, lit well and doing real work, alongside our product and the slides they build in it. We use photography because professionals' work is real. We support it with icons and lightweight spot illustrations drawn from our product. We never use cartoons or abstract illustration, because they make professionals' work look imagined.

Art direction

Photography

Principles

We hold every photograph to 1 principle and 9 rules.

Always in the light

We light the professional, literally and figuratively.

We use light to spotlight the professional in their element. Their face is turned toward the light, and the light can fall off into shadow or wash across the space around them, as long as the eye lands on the person first.

Every image tells an editorial story about the new professional way to present, with pros confident, prepared and at ease in their work. Every action and lifestyle shot is candid.

The professional is the hero and the product is implied, never shown. We shoot the way a trusted co-worker sees you: from across the table, mid-conversation, caught rather than posed.

The rules

These hold for every image and every shot type. Break one and the image doesn't ship.

01
Light

Turn the professional’s face toward the light so it spotlights them. Let the light fall off or wash the space around them as the setting needs.

02
Camera

Shoot from seated table height past a soft foreground object, such as a glass, a mug or a colleague’s shoulder, so we feel in the room.

03
Gaze

In close-up portraits, the professional can look into the lens or stay in the moment, whichever serves the story. In every other shot, they look at a colleague or the task and never at the lens.

04
Together shots

Show 2 or more colleagues with 1 hero, and let the light favor the professional the story is about.

05
Wardrobe

Dress professionals in current, tailored workwear in quiet neutrals, and vary the fabric and cut from shot to shot. Add 1 small accent in a shade or tint of Agency Azul or Ready Rose that suits the light and mood, taking no more than 10% of the frame.

06
Devices

A laptop can sit in frame as part of the work. Its screen is readable only when the product is the focus of the story, and then it shows a real screenshot placed in post.

07
Props and words

Show coffee or tea, never alcohol. Our images never contain writing or words, including signage, logos and numbers.

08
Composition

Hold a single subject with room to breathe, and place the eye line on a third.

09
Range

Cast wide across role, age, geography, ability and body size, so every professional sees their team in our frames. We show heritage only through real features, never through cultural jewelry, dress, decor or props.

Before it ships
Every answer is yes. One no and the image goes back.

    

      

        
    
          
  • Is the primary subject's face the brightest, warmest skin in the frame?
    • 
          
  • Are they facing the light rather than backlit or silhouetted?
    • 
          
  • Does the light spotlight them first, without a glowing halo around their head?
    • 
          
  • Outside close-up portraits, are they looking at a person or a task rather than the lens?
    • 
          
  • Is there a blurred foreground element?
    • 
          
  • Is there 1 accent in a shade of Azul or Rose, taking no more than 10% of the frame?
    • 
          
  • Is any laptop secondary to the person, with a readable screen only in product shots?
    • 
          
  • Is the frame free of writing, words and logos, and is there no alcohol?
    • 
          
  • Do hands, eyes and faces hold up at 100%, with real pores, crisp highlights and no skin smoothing?
    • 
          
  • Does the image tell a story about the new professional way to present?
    • 
        

        
Check the spotlight first on every new batch. Generators either light the whole room evenly or overdo the light into an aura.

        
When an image passes every check, add it to the approved images library so the next professional can build on it.

Iconography

  

    

      

        Icons come from Material Symbols, outlined and lightly rounded. No
        emoji, no hand-drawn glyphs. Where a single icon can't explain the
        idea, we draw a spot illustration in the same vocabulary — never a
        third style.
      

      
Go to Material Symbols ↗

    


    

      

        
Style

        
Outlined, rounded variant. Optical size 24. Stroke weight 400.

      

      

        
Sizes

        
24px is the standard. 20px in dense UI. 48px for feature icons.

      

      

        
Color

        
Inherit text color by default. Cyan only when calling for action.

Art direction
Motion

  

    
We use motion to show professionals what changed and where to look next. Every movement is subtle and functional, so it never slows down the work of building a deck.
Download the motion templates ↓

    

      
Duration
Interface feedback runs at 0.2s. Image crossfades run at 1.2s so they never pull attention from the page.

      
Easing
We use a standard ease on every transition. We never use bounce, overshoot or spring effects.

      
States
Hover darkens the color, and press scales the element to 98%. Nothing moves that the professional did not touch.
